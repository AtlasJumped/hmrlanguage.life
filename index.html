Writing clearly

A model of reality is only as clear as the words that carry it. This site lists the habits we keep, the phrases we cut, where commas go, what can be shortened, and the working terms we use because the usual words say too little.

The rules this site writes by

Form

Short sentences. One idea in each.

Serial comma: bones, fascia, and skin.

No en dash. Use a comma, a colon, or a new sentence.

No sentence starts with the word And.

Titles carry no closing period.

Voice

American English: color, center, behavior, organized.

We, never I.

Plain words over long ones.

No remark about how careful or honest the page is being. Be careful and honest, and leave the remark out.

Claims

Mark each claim once, where it is made, as established science, HMR's view, or open.

Established science carries a source link.

HMR's view reads as HMR's: In HMR, or HMR holds.

Open means we do not know yet. Say so in those words.

Cut these

Words that add length and no meaning. Each pair below is an invented example.

Filler

Before: It is really quite important to note that fascia is basically everywhere in the body.

After: Fascia is everywhere in the body.

Words like really, quite, basically, very, and just rarely carry meaning. Cut them first.

Stacked hedges

Before: It would seem that this may perhaps suggest a possible link.

After: This may be linked.

One hedge is enough. If the claim has a label, the label does the work.

Caveats that sound automatic

Before: This is the model's premise, not an established result. We state it plainly, and readers should weigh it accordingly.

After: In HMR, Universal Intelligence comes first.

The words In HMR already say whose claim it is. A second sentence that repeats the point adds nothing.

In which and by which chains

Before: The process by which the body distributes load is one in which every part is linked to the others.

After: The body distributes load through linked parts.

When a sentence needs in which twice, split it, or find the verb hiding inside the noun.

Announcing

Before: In this section we will now go on to discuss the three main kinds of exercise.

After: Exercise comes in three main kinds.

Do the thing. Do not say that you are about to.

Empty openers

Before: It is worth pointing out that there are many people who stretch every day.

After: Many people stretch every day.

There is, there are, and it is often push the real subject to the end.

Where a comma goes, and where it does not

Use a comma

In a list of three or more, before and or or: bones, fascia, and skin.

Between two full sentences joined by and, but, or so: The model is simple, and it can be tested.

After a long opening phrase: When the body is loaded unevenly, the weak line fails first.

Around extra information that can be dropped: Fascia, which wraps every muscle, holds its shape under load.

In numbers of four digits or more: 1,000 veterans.

Leave it out

Between a subject and its verb.

When the second half has no subject of its own: The model is simple and can be tested.

After a short opening phrase: Today we test it.

Before a clause the sentence needs: The fascia that wraps the muscle is thickest at the knee.

Between two sentences with nothing joining them. Use a period or a colon.

The which and that test

If the clause can be removed and the sentence still says the same thing, it takes which and commas. If removing it changes what is meant, it takes that and none.

Say it in fewer words

Each long form below can be replaced by the short form after the arrow with no loss of meaning.

  • in order to: to
  • due to the fact that, owing to the fact that: because
  • at this point in time, at the present time: now
  • in the event that: if
  • has the ability to, is able to: can
  • make use of, utilize: use
  • a number of: several, or give the number
  • prior to: before
  • with regard to, in relation to: about
  • in spite of the fact that: although
  • each and every: each
  • first and foremost: first
  • whether or not: whether
  • it is important to note that: drop it, and say the thing

HMR's working terms

These are HMR's choices of word, not rules of English. We use them because each carries more than the common word it replaces.

HMR's view

Original and deviation

Instead of change. Change says only that something is different. Deviation says more: there was a standard, the original, and something departed from it. That makes the departure something we can measure and, where possible, close.

The knee shows a deviation from its original alignment tells the reader what the reference is. The knee has changed does not.

HMR's view

System, situation, conditions

System is for parts that act together, in place of the vague thing or stuff. Situation is for how things stand at one moment. Conditions is for the circumstances that have to hold for something to happen.

Choose the one that fits, and keep it fixed.

Open

Still open

English has gaps, and this list is short. If you keep reaching for a word that does not exist, write to us. A good new word names something we already need to say.
